## Notes in the margin

A footnote is written the usual way, with a marker in the text and the note anywhere below.[^margin] On a wide screen the note stands in the margin, level with its marker. On a phone the marker becomes a button that unfolds the note in place.

Notes can carry **emphasis**, `code` and links, and a second one nearby simply stacks under the first.[^stack]

[^margin]: Like this one. A margin note keeps the eye on the page instead of sending it to the bottom and back, a habit borrowed from Tufte's books [@tufte2006beautiful].

[^stack]: Written as `[^stack]` in the text and `[^stack]: …` on its own line.

## Citations

Sources are listed once, in the `bibliography` of the frontmatter, and cited by key: low-rank adaptation [@hu2021lora] builds on the Transformer [@vaswani2017attention]. Several keys can share one marker [@hu2021lora; @tufte2006beautiful].

The first time a work is cited its details appear in the margin. Later citations show only the number, which opens the reference when clicked. A numbered reference list is added to the end of the post automatically.

## Code

Fenced code gets syntax colours for both themes, a language label and a copy button.

```python
def lora_update(W, A, B, scale):
    """Low-rank update: W' = W + scale * B @ A."""
    return W + scale * (B @ A)
```

## Math

Formulas are written in LaTeX. Inline math such as $W' = W + \gamma BA$ sits in the line, and display math gets a line of its own:

$$
\mathcal{L}(\theta) = -\frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} \log p_\theta(y_i \mid x_i)
$$

Environments work as they do in LaTeX. `align` and `equation` number themselves, and `\tag` sets a label by hand:

$$
\begin{align}
h &= W_0 x + \Delta W x \\
  &= W_0 x + \frac{\alpha}{r} B A x
\end{align}
$$

$$
\operatorname{softmax}(z)_i = \frac{e^{z_i}}{\sum_j e^{z_j}} \tag{$\star$}
$$

Matrices and cases:

$$
A = \begin{bmatrix} a_{11} & a_{12} \\ a_{21} & a_{22} \end{bmatrix},
\qquad
f(x) = \begin{cases} x & x \ge 0 \\ \alpha x & x < 0 \end{cases}
$$

Math also works inside notes[^math] and tables. Copying a formula from the page gives its LaTeX source.

[^math]: For example $\lVert \Delta W \rVert_F \le \epsilon$, set in the margin.

## Foldable blocks

Material that not every reader needs, such as a derivation, a proof or a long aside, can be folded away behind its title and opened with a click. It is written with HTML's own `<details>`, with a blank line between the tags and the text:

```html
<details>
<summary>Title</summary>

Text, math, tables, code…

</details>
```

<details>
<summary>Derivation: why the mean minimises squared error</summary>

Let $f(c) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} (x_i - c)^2$. Differentiate with respect to $c$ and set the derivative to zero:

$$
f'(c) = -2 \sum_{i=1}^{n} (x_i - c) = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
c = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} x_i . \tag{1}
$$

Notes and citations inside a folded block stay out of the margin and open in place when their marker is clicked.[^fold]

</details>
